The first entry on isitslop.org is isitslop.org. Its grade on day one was 100% sloppy, and it is 95% as I write this. People keep asking whether that is a bug. It is not. It is the whole point.

## What the number says

Two things are measured, each out of 100, and the rules are [public and generated from the code](/methodology).

**Quality** asks whether this is real, maintained work by more than one person over time. It reads the repository for signals you cannot buy: how many of the last 52 weeks had a commit, scaled by how old the repo is; how many other humans have put in two or more commits; whether anyone has ever filed an issue. Stars, forks, badges and READMEs are not used, because they are the first thing a sloppy project fakes.

**Popularity** asks whether the people who find it actually use it. It reads the owner's own Google Analytics: active users in the last 30 days, and how many came back. Under a hundred users is zero reach, however loyal those users are, because from the outside "nobody there" and "nobody there yet" look the same.

Average the two, subtract from 100, and you have how sloppy it is.

## Why we are slop

On the day this site launched, its repository was a few days old, one person had committed to it, nobody had filed an issue, and nobody had visited. Quality: 0. Popularity: 0. Sloppy: 100%.

Every one of those readings was true. A thing that is a week old, built by one person, used by nobody, is indistinguishable from the thousand other things built last week by one person and used by nobody, most of which will be abandoned by spring. That is what slop means here: not bad, but not yet proven to be worth anything.

## Why we will not fix it by hand

It would take one line to exempt our own domain. We will not write it, and if someone sends it as a pull request we will decline it in public.

The rules are the product. If they bend for the people who wrote them, they are worth nothing for anyone else. Our number moves the only way yours does: other people putting work into the repository, and people coming back to the site. If that never happens, the number will say so, and it will be right.

## How to argue with it

If you think a rule is wrong, the rule is [a pull request away](/contribute). Every grade can be [appealed in public](/about), and an appeal that points at a rule misfiring gets the rule fixed for everyone, never a private exception. That is the only kind of fairness we know how to make.

Watch the number.
